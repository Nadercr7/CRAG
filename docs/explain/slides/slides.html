3How it works

The verifier reads question and paragraph together

One small cross-encoder, MiniLM-L6. It sees the question and one paragraph as a single input and returns a probability. Bars are real scores for the top eight paragraphs of the documentary question: the two gold paragraphs score 0.595 and 0.441, the distractors are near zero. Press for calibration: expected calibration error falls from 0.085 to 0.005, so a score means roughly what it says.
3How it works

Two thresholds sort every question

The decision uses the best paragraph score. Above 0.65 the evidence is correct, below 0.15 it is incorrect, in between it is ambiguous. Ambiguous and incorrect both trigger a rewrite and one more search. Press to see the other four example questions: none reached 0.65, so each one triggered a rewrite.
3How it works

Trimming sends about a fifth of the words

Each paragraph is split into sentences and the verifier scores each one. Sentences at 0.05 or above stay, the rest go. In this example, 599 words become 99. Over the whole test set, context falls from about 1,070 to about 217 tokens. Be ready for the cost slide later: trimming sometimes cuts the sentence that held the answer.
3How it works

The scale decides: enough to answer, or stop

A second small model, MiniLM-L12, reads the question and the trimmed context and returns the probability that it is sufficient. Below 0.90 the system abstains. On its dev proxy task it separates sufficient from missing-hop at AUC 0.978 and from no-evidence at 0.998. Press for the Guildford example: 0.618, so the line stops. Press again for the AUC numbers. Caveat: AUC measures the proxy task and says little about insufficiency in general.
4Building the verifier

Fine-tuning broke ranking outside HotpotQA

Training data: HotpotQA passages and sentences plus MS MARCO pairs, starting from a MiniLM-L6 cross-encoder. HotpotQA ranking improved, but SciFact nDCG@10 fell from 69.6 to 53.3. A nonsense query against medical abstracts scored +0.6 where the base model gave -9.6. Cause: HotpotQA distractors share words with the question, and real second-hop paragraphs share almost none. The model learned that word overlap means irrelevant.
4Building the verifier

Averaging the weights brought it back

WiSE-FT: average the fine-tuned weights with the base weights. Alpha 0 is the base model, alpha 1 is the full fine-tune. We fixed the rule before reading the table: the largest alpha whose out-of-domain dev mean stays within 0.01 of the base. That is 0.7. It keeps most of the HotpotQA gain and brings SciFact back to 68.0. You can click the alpha buttons.
4Building the verifier

Three more things that went wrong

Three lessons from the report. One: our dev check compared the wrong candidates and made the broken model look better, so we rebuilt it to match the test protocol. Two: one threshold was doing two jobs, which cost us supporting facts. Three: the teacher filter threw away most of the training data. Each one changed the design.
5How we tested it fairly

One question, three kinds of evidence

Every test question is run three ways. Supportive: the gold paragraphs are in the pool. Missing: they are removed, so saying I don't know is the right behaviour. Misleading: the answer entity inside the gold text is swapped for another of the same type, and repeating it counts as planted. 300 plus 300 plus 239 gives 839 pairs.
5How we tested it fairly

Dev to choose, test to judge, once

Questions come from one HotpotQA pool of 4,969 paragraphs: 200 dev, 300 test, sampled with seed 13. Every choice was made on dev. The abstain threshold followed a rule written into PLAN.md before the dev answers existed. Test ran once at the end, with paired bootstrap over questions, 10,000 resamples.
5How we tested it fairly

Seven arms, one station added at a time

The ablation ladder. A is plain hybrid RAG. Each arm adds one thing, so we can see what every station buys. F is the full system. G is a variant of F with a single threshold, so it sits beside F rather than above it. Same generator, same prompts for every arm.
5How we tested it fairly

The free tier ran out, so generation moved

Groq's free tier caps each model at 200,000 tokens a day. At about 1,000 tokens per call, the 5,419 distinct dev and test prompts would have taken close to four weeks. We moved generation to Kaggle with vLLM and an open model. Identical prompts are generated once: 5,873 test traces became 3,267 prompts.

Abstaining costs some correct answers

The price. On supportive questions the full system answers 62% of the time against 73%, and its correct rate falls by 5 points, with an interval that touches zero. The curve shows selective accuracy as the abstain threshold sweeps. Plain RAG sits to the right and lower. Curve and both points are for supportive questions only. Our operating point is at 0.90.
6Results

A case where the gate cost us the answer

Here is a case we lost. Plain RAG answered Geraldine Page correctly. In our system the sentence that holds the answer scored 0.049, just under the 0.05 cut, so the context was thin, sufficiency came out at 0.002, and the system said I don't know. This is the same effect as the 57% of supportive questions where trimming keeps every supporting fact.
6Results

A second model checked our metric

An LLM from another family graded 100 answered test questions. It agreed with our strict metric on 85, kappa 0.71. All 15 disagreements went one way: the judge accepted an alias that the metric rejected. So our hallucination rates are overstated for every arm.
7Limits and next

What it does not do

Four limits. Planted answers mostly get through. The rewrite step did nothing measurable. Everything is trained on HotpotQA, and the verifier is slightly worse out of domain than its base. The metric is strict, so absolute hallucination rates are overstated.
7Limits and next

It ships with a UI that shows every decision

The product side. The pipeline explorer shows 22 real traces with plain RAG next to the full system, and a live box. The results dashboard reads the metrics files. Every request produces a trace with scores, decision, kept and dropped sentences and timings.
7Limits and next

What we would do next

Each next step answers one limit. Detect fluent but altered paragraphs with a different signal. Drop or rework the rewrite step. Train and test beyond HotpotQA, with more seeds. Replace the strict metric with an alias-aware one and add a claim-level faithfulness check.
7Limits and next
Close on the line. Recap: fewer hallucinations in all three conditions, about a fifth of the context, and a price of about 5 points of correct answers. Then open for questions.
